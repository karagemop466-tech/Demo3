Topics: request vs what the exchange actually offers

The five topics asked for are not five fields on Kalshi. The mapping below is the only defensible one: each topic is bound to the exchange's own category and tag vocabulary, read from GET /search/tags_by_categories, and each row states whether that binding matched live series when used. Where a requested topic has no official counterpart (gas prices, culture) we say so instead of quietly substituting.

Two structural gaps, stated up front. ① There is no Culture category in the official taxonomy — the closest published buckets are Entertainment, Mentions and Social; “Person of the Year” (KXTIME) was found by tag search, not category, and is used as the culture anchor. ② There is no retail-gasoline category: the official taxonomy has Commodities with the Oil & Gas tag, and the crude-oil series (KXWTIW, WTI weekly) is what the filter returns. Ordinary-U.S.-gasoline-price markets would be an Economics + Oil and energy hit, and in this capture that secondary selector returned no open series. Both facts are FLAG-03, not silent judgement calls.

How a market gets a topic label in this project

OrderRuleWhy
1Series ticker present in the verified universe built from GET /series?category=…&tags=… → use that topic The selector came from the exchange's own taxonomy, so membership is authoritative. Marked series-verified.
2Otherwise, a match against a topic's documented ticker prefix (e.g. KXPGATOUR) → same topic, flagged provisional Covers series that exist but did not answer the category query. Marked provisional everywhere it is used, including in rankings.
3Otherwise → excluded from the topic tables and listed under “outside the five topics” No keyword inference from titles. A mislabelled row is worse than a missing row.

Implemented identically in scripts/collect.py, scripts/aggregate_capture.py and site/assets/api.js so the site and the collector cannot drift apart.
