Live events & resolution tracking

Every tracked market stays on this page from status=open until the exchange marks it settled, and the outcome is recorded from the fields Kalshi actually returns — not from a guess about what “probably happened”. A market that resolves to YES pays notional_value_dollars per contract to YES holders; NO pays the same to NO holders; only net positions settle (market settlement).

Tracking loop (what the code does) 
GET /events?status=open&min_updated_ts=…   # cheap change poll
  └─ GET /markets?event_ticker=E&status=open
       ├─ record prices, sizes, depth, volume_fp
       ├─ when status ∉ {open, active}  → poll on
       └─ when result ∈ {yes,no}        → append to
                                           data/resolutions.json

The collector is scripts/collect.py --resolutions-only; the ledger is append-only and keyed by market ticker, so a resolution can never be overwritten by a later run. In the browser, tick “Track in this tab” to keep the same watchlist in localStorage and refresh it on the interval you set above.

Why a “live event” can still show zero liquidity

Empty book ≠ dead market

A brand-new strike legitimately has no resting orders: KXPRESPERSON-28-ESLO was created 2026-11-19 and returned bid 0.0000 / ask 0.0000, volume 0.00 — real zeroes, not missing data.

Empty book ≠ typo

The orderbook endpoint answers 200 with empty arrays for an unknown ticker, so a mistake is indistinguishable without a second call to GET /markets/{ticker}.

Early close is normal

Sampled markets carry can_close_early=true and early_close_condition, e.g. “close and expire after a winner is declared” — so trading can stop long before close_time.
